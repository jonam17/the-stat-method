# Key takeaways review

The box at the top of each article — its key takeaways and closing line — rewritten in a warmer,
plainer voice. **The rule: wording may change, claims may not.** Every takeaway restates something
the article's body already says, and the body carries the citation; no takeaway adds a claim. Each
row below names the section a takeaway restates, so it can be checked against it.

Voice: speak to the reader as "you"; lead with what to do; plain words over jargon ("rehearse the
lift", not "rehearsing the movement pattern"); keep the numbers that matter; one idea per point,
each a complete claim.

Where a takeaway said something its own article does not support, it was corrected to match the
body, and that is listed under **Corrections**. Where the body contradicted itself, the sentence
that disagreed with the rest of its article was corrected — listed separately, because it changes
reviewed body text.

Rewritten for voice only; each point restates the takeaway it replaces, which were verified in

### Corrections

- **One-rep max — takeaway.** "Rep-max formulas are curve fits to population data, not laws."
  The article says most "were never derived from research" — they began as charts and tables and
  were extrapolated afterwards. The new takeaway says so.
- **RPE — takeaway.** "Novices systematically underestimate how many reps they have left." The
  article says a meta-analysis of 12 studies found **no overall effect of training experience**, that
  almost everyone under-predicts by about one rep, and that "novices are worse" is "plausible and
  unproven". The new takeaway says that.
- **Strength standards — takeaway.** "…nothing about your potential, your health, or how long you
  have trained." The article never mentions training age there; it says potential, health, work ethic
  and whether training is well designed. The new takeaway matches.
