## Lat pulldown

**[1] Li Q, Yan J, Qiao M, Quan J, Chen Y, Gong M, Niu W, Wang L. Eur J Sport Sci 2025;25(1):e12243.** https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsc.12243 (PMID 39716392)

Read: full text on PMC (PMC11667758). Funding: Shanghai Education Science Research Project (No. C2023195) and
the National Natural Science Foundation of China (No. 12272272). Conflicts: "All the authors disclosed no
relevant relationships."

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 34 recreationally active male students, randomised to two pulldown groups | Methods ("Thirty-four healthy recreationally active male college students were randomly assigned") |
| ★ Three times a week, eight weeks, 4 × 12, load 65% → 80% 1RM in 5% steps every two weeks | Methods, training protocol |
| ★ Standard-machine group: peak pulling force +27.9%; no difference between groups | Results ("significant main effect of time … p < 0.001"; no group or interaction effect); 24.1% and 27.9% by group |
| ★ Pull-ups 2.18 → 5.82 in the standard-machine group | Results ("from 2.18 ± 1.42 and 2.18 ± 1.51 … to 8.47 ± 3.54 and 5.82 ± 3.36") |
| Strength test was an isometric maximal pull (MVIC) | Methods/Results ("lat pull-down MVIC peak force") |
| No muscle-size measure; anthropometry unchanged; no non-training control | Methods (two training arms only); Results (anthropometry not significant) |
| Grip 10–20 cm wider than shoulders; bar to below the chin, in front of the head; neutral head (how-to steps) | Methods ("A pronated grip, 10–20 cm wider than shoulder width"; "pulled down in front of the head until reaching beneath the chin"; "Maintaining a neutral head position") |

The entry uses the standard-machine (TRT) group throughout. The other group trained with elastic bands on the
bar and a lowered seat, which is not what the page describes.

**[2] Paoli A, Pacelli QF, Cancellara P, Toniolo L, Moro T, Canato M, Miotti D, Neri M, Morra A, Quadrelli M, Reggiani C. Nutrients 2016;8(6):331.** https://doi.org/10.3390/nu8060331

Read: full text on mdpi.com. Funding: "grants from ASI (WP 1B235) (Agenzia Spaziale Italiana—Italian Space
Agency)". Conflicts: "The authors declare no conflict of interest."

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 18 young men, no regular resistance training for at least two years | Methods ("None of them had been engaged in resistance training on a regular basis for at least two years") |
| ★ Eight weeks; six exercises — bench press, lat pulldowns, seated rows, shoulder press, hammer curls, lying dumbbell external rotation | Methods ("The exercises performed throughout the program were: bench press, latissimus pulldowns, seated rows, …") |
| ★ Lat muscle fibres grew | Results (fibre CSA, "main effect: p = 0.006"); biopsy "from the lateral edge of the LDM at the level of the 5th rib" |
| Pulldown 1RM about +17% | Results ("from 670.8 ± 170 N to 785 ± 182 N; p < 0.0001"); 785 / 670.8 = 1.170 |

Doubt: the paper's main question is protein intake. Fibre growth was significant as a training effect across
both diet groups (p = 0.006, no diet interaction), but one passage reports the fibre-size increase as
significant only in the normal-protein group. The entry says only that fibres grew with training, which
both readings support. "Only pulling exercises" is our description: the external-rotation exercise trains
the rotator cuff and does not pull.

## Pull-up

**[1] Sánchez-Moreno M, Cornejo-Daza PJ, González-Badillo JJ, Pareja-Blanco F. J Strength Cond Res 2020;34(4):911–917.** https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000003500 (PMID 32213783)

Read: the abstract in the University of Seville repository record (idus.us.es, handle 11441/162727). The DOI
and PMID are from the Universidad Pablo de Olavide record (investiga.upo.es). The publisher page and PubMed
were not reachable. Funding declaration not read.

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 29 strength-trained men, randomised to stop sets at 25% or 50% velocity loss (15 / 14) | Abstract ("strength-trained men"; "VL25 (n = 15) or VL50") |
| ★ Eight weeks, 16 sessions, body-mass prone-grip (overhand) pull-ups | Abstract ("an 8-week (16 sessions) velocity-based body mass (BM) prone PU training program") |
| ★ VL25 improved in every measure, incl. estimated 1RM and maximum reps; VL50 did not change | Abstract ("VL25 improved significantly (P<0.001) in all the evaluated variables while VL50 remained unchanged") |
| Strength and endurance only, no muscle size | Abstract (outcome list) |

Doubt: the repository's abstract text is garbled at the participant count ("Twenty-nine nine"). 29 matches
the group sizes (15 + 14). A figure after the body mass ("15.9 ± 4.9 PU") may be the baseline pull-up count,
but it was too garbled to use, so the entry does not give one.

**[2] Sepehri Rahnama H, Ganji S, Vadasz K, Prokai J. Sports 2025;13(12):433.** https://doi.org/10.3390/sports13120433

Read: full text on mdpi.com. Funding: "This research received no external funding." Conflicts: "The authors
declare no conflict of interest."

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 30 physically inactive young men, three groups of 10, twice weekly for eight weeks | Methods |
| ★ All groups: assisted pull-ups, inverted rows, reverse-grip pull-ups, negative pull-ups; plus running intervals, forearm or core work | Methods, training programs |
| ★ Pull-ups rose in every group: 4.30 → 7.00, 4.00 → 12.90, 2.70 → 6.50 | Results |
| Every group had an add-on; no untrained control | Methods (three training groups only) |

The paper's question is the add-ons (forearm work did best). The entry uses it only to show that the shared
pull-up routine came with gains in every group, and says it cannot be credited to the routine alone. Authors
note no a priori power analysis.

## Seated cable row

**[1] Paoli 2016** — as under Lat pulldown [2]. The entry claims only that lat fibres grew in a program whose
pulling exercises were the seated row and the pulldown, and says that the trial cannot separate them and
that no trial testing the seated row alone was found.
