## [1] WHO/FAO/UNU. Protein and amino acid requirements in human nutrition. WHO TRS 935, 2007

Link: https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/43411 (from a search result on screen). Series number, year and
publisher from the report's cataloguing page. ISBN 92 4 120935 6 is on that page but is not used.

**Checks.** Exists: yes. Type: report of a joint expert consultation held in 2002, published 2007; the
cataloguing page says it "does not necessarily represent the decisions or the stated policy" of the
three bodies, so the article calls it "the expert report". Population: healthy adults (requirement
estimates); the adult pattern also applies to older adults (§8.5). Number provenance: all requirement
values from Table 23; the 70 kg figures are the drafting session's arithmetic (39 × 70 = 2,730 mg;
184 × 70 = 12,880 mg). Funding: not stated in the parts read. Superseded: in part. §6.3 leaves an
updated table of scores to a later report (the FAO 2013 report, [9]). Its requirement values are still
the reference ones that [7] and [4] use.

**Internal inconsistency found:** Table 49 (§14.5) gives phenylalanine + tyrosine as 30 mg/g protein.
Table 23 gives 38, and 25 ÷ 0.66 = 37.9. The article does not use this value.

## [2] Wolfe RR. J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2017;14:30

Link: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0184-9 (DOI on the PDF's first page).

**Checks.** Exists: yes. Type: brief narrative review by one author. Population: human studies only,
by the author's choice. Rat data are discussed and set aside. Number provenance: the two infusion
studies are Louard et al. 1990 and 1995 (his refs 13–14), not read here; the article reports only that
synthesis fell, as he describes. Funding: "Not applicable". **Competing interests:** grants or honoraria
from the National Cattleman's Beef Checkoff program, Abbott Nutrition, Danone and PepsiCo; owns shares
in Essential Blends, LLC; consultant to Axcella LLC. The article names the first two, with "a beef
checkoff programme" for the first, and does not say what Essential Blends sells (the paper doesn't).
Superseded: in part. Jackman 2017 ([10]) measured oral BCAAs alone the same year (+22% vs placebo);
the article reports both.

**Internal inconsistency found:** the theoretical maximum rise in synthesis from BCAAs is "about 30%"
in the abstract, "could not exceed 15%" in the text, and "20%" in the Fig. 1 legend. The article uses
none of these figures.

## [10] Jackman SR et al. Front Physiol 2017;8:390

Link: https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2017.00390 (DOI on the PDF's first page). Trial ISRCTN98737111.

**Checks.** Exists: yes. Type: randomised, counter-balanced crossover trial, participants blinded
(drinks from GSK). Eleven enrolled; 10 completed both trials (one withdrew through illness).
Population: healthy, resistance-trained young men (20.1 ± 1.3 years, about 83 kg), fed a high-protein
breakfast 3 h before exercise. The article says "after breakfast". Number provenance: 22% (0.110 vs
0.090 %/h) from Results; "about half" is the authors' comparison with earlier whey studies (Churchward-

"earlier studies". **Statistics:** myofibrillar MPS was tested with a one-tailed paired t-test
(P = 0.012); the article calls the trial small and doesn't describe the test. **Funding:**
GlaxoSmithKline Healthcare research grant to Tipton; drinks made by GSK; authors declare no conflict of
interest. Same group as [5] (Witard, Jackman, Tipton). Superseded: it qualifies [2]. The article now
reports both: [2] found no oral study and [10] measured one.
