## Barbell bench press

**[1] Gavanda S, Wischtukat D, Ruckdeschel M, Geisler S, Held S. Biol Sport 2026;43:839–846.** https://doi.org/10.5114/biolsport.2026.157993

Read: full text on termedia.pl (the publisher). Funding: "No financial support"; conflicts: none declared.
The device tested (an elastic bench press support) is commercial, but no author or funder tie to a maker is
declared.

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 22 trained men, each with at least a year of bench press experience | Methods, Subjects ("minimum of 1 year of experience with the bench press exercise"); Table 1 |
| ★ Three times a week for ten weeks, powerlifting-style, with or without the device | Methods, Research design and Resistance training program |
| ★ Chest thickness rose in both groups, ~17% with the bar alone; no group difference | Results ("RAW … 17.2 ± 13.2%"); Table 3 |
| Triceps thickness did not change significantly | Results; Table 3 (time p = 0.071) |
| No non-training control; program also had rows, chin-ups, squats, deadlifts | Research design (two groups only); Table 2 |
| Every rep touched the chest and finished with elbows straight (how-to step) | Resistance training program ("ensuring chest contact and full elbow extension") |

Doubts: Table 3 labels the 1-RM row "% BM" while its values match the kilograms in Table 1, and its body-mass
values for the two groups are swapped relative to Table 1. Neither number is used in the entry. The discussion
calls the design "randomized controlled"; the methods describe a matched-pair parallel design with no
untrained control — the entry says there was no control group.

## Push-up

**[1] Kikuchi N, Nakazato K. J Exerc Sci Fit 2017;15(1):37–42.** PMID 29541130 (PubMed page), DOI 10.1016/j.jesf.2017.06.003.

Read: the full PubMed abstract. The PMC full text was behind a captcha; "untrained" and "performed to failure"
are from the paper's own statement of purpose as shown in the PMC search result, not the abstract. Funding
declaration not read.

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 18 young men, twice weekly, 8 weeks; bench press at 40% 1RM vs push-ups adjusted (e.g. kneeling) to the same load | Abstract (Methods) |
| ★ Both groups increased pectoralis major and triceps thickness and bench press 1RM | Abstract (Results) |
| Untrained; to failure | Introduction, statement of purpose (PMC text in search result) |

Doubt: the abstract prints identical pectoralis values for both groups (17.0 → 20.8 mm), which looks like a

## Smith machine incline press

**[1] Chaves SFN, Rocha-Júnior VA, Encarnação IGA, Martins-Costa HC, Freitas EDS, Coelho DB, Franco FSC,
Loenneke JP, Bottaro M, Ferreira-Júnior JB. Int J Exerc Sci 2020;13(6):859–872.** https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijes/vol13/iss6/12

Read: full text, from the journal's own PDF. **No funding or conflict-of-interest statement appears in the
paper** — recorded as absent, not as a pass.

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 47 untrained men; once weekly, 8 weeks; flat, incline at 44°, or both; equal sets | Abstract; Methods (Protocol, Resistance training protocol) |
| ★ **All pressing on a Smith machine** — hence the entry's name | Methods ("All bench press exercise was completed on a Smith Machine") |
| ★ Muscle measured in 30 of the 47 | Methods ("30 subjects for the muscle thickness data") |
| ★ Incline grew more at the second intercostal space only; the other two sites did not differ | Abstract; Results; Table 3 |
| Second intercostal space is the highest of three sites, under the clavicle midpoint | Methods, Muscle thickness (2nd, 3rd and 5th intercostal spaces, "all under the clavicle midpoint") |
| Isometric bench press strength rose similarly in all groups | Abstract; Results; Table 2 |
| Every rep touched the chest and finished with elbows straight (how-to step) | Methods ("touch their chest … elbows fully extended") |

Note: the incline group started thicker at that site (15.1 vs 11.9 mm); the authors adjusted for baseline
(ANCOVA). Untrained, once-weekly training is a narrow basis — the entry names the population and the machine.

## Considered and left out

- **Dumbbell bench press vs dumbbell fly (JSCR, accepted 2026).** A ten-week trial in recreationally trained men
  reporting similar chest growth from both. Only an author's post and secondary write-ups were reachable; the
  authors, DOI and abstract were never on screen from a primary source. Revisit once it is published — it would
  support a dumbbell fly entry and add muscle-size evidence to the dumbbell bench press.
- **Smoak 2023 (J Strength Perform), barbell vs dumbbell bench press.** Rejected: the abstract and the methods
  describe different participants (mean age 18.1 vs 22.7 years, 97.4 vs 71.2 kg), and the stated inclusion age of
  20–30 is incompatible with the abstract's mean. A paper that cannot say who it studied fails population fit.
- **Ogasawara 2012 (Interv Med Appl Sci), 24 weeks of bench press.** Seven men; its results were not readable from
  any page reached. Gavanda 2026 covers the barbell bench press with a larger, trained sample.
- **Schwanbeck 2020** (already cited for the Smith machine squat) also included a Smith bench press. Not needed: the
  machine slot went to the incline press, which has a chest-specific trial.
