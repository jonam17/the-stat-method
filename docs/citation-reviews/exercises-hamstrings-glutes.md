3D ultrasound in Morin) — a stronger evidence base than back or chest. Four of five trials had a non-training

in Plotkin 2023; the other entries say "hamstrings" and list glutes as secondary without a size claim.

## Hip thrust

**[1] Plotkin DL, Rodas MA, Vigotsky AD, et al. Front Physiol 2023;14:1279170.** https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170

Read: full text on frontiersin.org; abstract on DOAJ. The author list ran to about twenty names and was
truncated on one page and duplicated a name on the other, so the reference uses "et al." Funding: gift funds
partly from the International Scientific Research Foundation for Fitness and Nutrition and from BC (Bret
Contreras), plus School of Kinesiology cost sharing; one author on an NIH T32 grant. Conflicts: "BC and MH"
disclose that they sell exercise-related products and services, and say they had no role beyond study design
and funding participant and MRI costs. The page states this plainly.

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 34 untrained adults completed: HT 5 men / 13 women, SQ 6 men / 10 women | Methods / Table 1 |
| ★ Nine weeks, twice weekly (15–17 sessions), 3 → 6 sets of 8–12 | Methods ("9 weeks of supervised training (15–17 sessions)") |
| ★ Glute growth similar between groups; estimates modestly favoured HT with appreciable variance | Abstract ("Gluteus mCSA increases were similar across both groups"; "modestly favored the HT … appreciable variance") |
| Gluteus medius + minimus: little or no growth | Abstract ("demonstrated little to no growth") |
| Squat grew quadriceps and adductors more; hamstrings did not grow in either group | Abstract; Discussion ("Hamstring mCSA changes did not occur in either group") |
| Each group gained most 3RM in its own lift; deadlift 3RM rose similarly | Abstract (3RM deadlift 0 ± 2 kg between groups) |
| Machine; feet 1–1.5× shoulder width; full rep = tibia vertical, femur parallel; plate briefly touches floor; ~2 s lowering (how-to) | Methods (Thruster 3.0; rep standard; tempo ~1 s / 2 s) |

Doubt: the paper reports between-group estimates only, not per-group significance. "Glute growth" on the page
rests on the abstract's "Gluteus mCSA increases". The bench-and-barbell setup in the first how-to step is the
common form; the trial used a hip-thrust machine, and the step says so.

## Seated leg curl and lying leg curl

**Maeo S, Huang M, Wu Y, Sakurai H, Kusagawa Y, Sugiyama T, Kanehisa H, Isaka T. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2021;53(4):825–837.** https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000002523 (seated [1], lying [1])

Read: full text on PMC (PMC7969179). Funding: Mizuno Sports Promotion Foundation research grant (2019-5).
Conflicts: none declared.

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 20 young adults, no systematic resistance training in the past 12 months | Methods ("none had been involved in any type of systematic resistance training program in the past 12 months") |
| ★ One leg seated (hip ~90°), the other prone (hip ~30°), randomised and counterbalanced | Methods |
| ★ 12 weeks, twice weekly, 5 × 10 at 70% 1RM | Methods |
| ★ Hamstrings volume +14.1% seated vs +9.3% prone; BFlh +14.4% vs +6.5% | Results (MRI) |
| 1RM change did not differ between legs | Results (z-scores 0.81 vs 0.76, P = 0.663) |

Sex was not reported in the text read, so the pages say "young adults".

standard seated leg curl, and the seated page says "a close relative … rather than the standard machine".
