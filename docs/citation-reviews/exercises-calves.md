## How the candidates were found

PubMed search run by the maintainer on 2026-11-15:
`(calf OR calves OR gastrocnemius OR soleus OR "calf raise" OR "heel raise") AND (hypertrophy OR "muscle thickness" OR "muscle volume") AND training`,
exported as abstracts (3,878 records; the term "calves" also returned veterinary papers, which were excluded).
A script kept records describing a multi-week training study or review with a muscle-size or strength
outcome; every remaining candidate that named calf or plantar-flexor training was then read as an abstract.

## Standing calf raise

**[1] Larsen S, Swinton PA, Sandberg NØ, Kristiansen BS, Fredriksen AB, Falch HN, van den Tillaar R, Wolf M. Front Psychol 2025;16:1494323.** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1494323

Read: full text on frontiersin.org. Funding: none ("no financial support was received"). Conflicts: none declared.

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|
| ★ 30 recruited, 23 analysed; untrained men (less than one session a week in the past 6 months), 31.8 ± 6.1 years | Methods |
| ★ Within-person: each leg randomly allocated to a condition | Methods |
| Unilateral standing Smith-machine calf raise, knee extended | Methods |
| ★ 10 weeks, twice weekly; 3 sets (weeks 2–5), 4 sets (weeks 6–9); 10–20 reps to momentary failure | Methods |
| ★ Medial gastrocnemius thickness: ordinary sets 20.4 → 21.8 mm (+6.7%); past-failure partials 20.6 → 22.5 mm (+9.6%) | Table 1 |
| Analysis favoured partials: 0.62 mm (95% CrI 0.21–1.0), BF 13.3 | Results |
| Only the medial gastrocnemius measured; no non-training control; untrained men only | Methods; Limitations |

Doubt: the abstract gives the study length as 10 weeks and the methods as "12 weeks"; the 12 counts the two
testing weeks (single sessions in weeks 1 and 11). The page says ten weeks of training.

**[2] Goli A, Attarieh P, Nunes JP, Khani S, Negahdar S, Fashi M, Ahmadizad S. Int J Exerc Sci 2026;19(4):4003.** https://doi.org/10.70252/IJES2026403

Read: full-text PDF at intjexersci.com (the PMC copy shows a bot check). Author list as printed on the article's
first page; PubMed's record orders and spells it differently ("Nehegadar S"). Funding and conflicts: no statement
in the article.

| Claim | Where |
|---|---|

| Plate-loaded standing calf-raise machine, one leg at a time, legs randomly allocated | Methods |
| ★ 10 weeks, twice weekly; ordinary leg 4 sets (weeks 1–6) then 6 sets (weeks 7–10) of 10–14RM to failure | Methods |
| ★ Other leg half the sets (2 then 3), each continued with partial reps from full dorsiflexion to a neutral (flat) foot, to a second failure | Methods |
| ~1 s up, 2 s down; full range from maximal dorsiflexion to peak plantarflexion (how-to) | Methods |
| ★ Medial gastrocnemius 20.3 → 21.7 mm (ordinary) and 19.9 → 21.3 mm (partials); lateral 20.4 → 22.0 and 21.0 → 22.6 mm — about 7% and 8% | Table 1 (percentages recalculated; the text says "8%" for averaged thickness) |

Doubts: the authors call the volume-load equated, but Table 1's totals differ (13.1 vs 11.8 tonnes, P = 0.392),

The page uses neither: it says only that both legs grew about the same with half the sets on one side.

## Seated calf raise — not drafted

| Source | Read | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Kataoka R, Vasenina E, Hammert WB, Ibrahim AH, Dankel SJ, Buckner SL. Eur J Appl Physiol 2022;122(3):623-634. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-021-04862-7 | Full-text PDF (maintainer), every page | 27 people who had trained their calves for 6+ months (21 men, 6 women), seated calf raise machine, 3 sessions a week for 6 weeks: one leg 4 sets of 8–12 to failure, the other low-load with blood-flow restriction, both followed by 4 × 20 body-weight standing calf raises. **No growth at any of three sites in either leg**; training loads rose (first set 24 → 43 kg in the heavy leg, Table 1). The authors note final-set effort ratings (~16 of 20) suggest sets may have stopped short of failure, and that 6 weeks may be too short. No funding; no conflicts |
| Van Every 2022; Plotkin 2022 | Full text | Seated calf raise always trained together with a straight-leg calf raise |

The only trial of the seated calf raise on its own found no growth, and the two that found growth always paired it
with another calf exercise. An entry would rest on a null result, so it is held. Decision 2026-11-17: leave it out until a trial of the seated calf raise on its own finds growth.
