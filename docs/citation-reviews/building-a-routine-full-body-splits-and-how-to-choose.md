## [1] Bull et al. 2020 — WHO guidelines

Bull FC, Al-Ansari SS, Biddle S, et al. Br J Sports Med 2020;54(24):1451–1462.
doi:10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955. PMID 33239350; PMCID PMC7719906.

1. **Exists:** yes; citation details confirmed on the PubMed Central page.
2. **Supports the claim:** yes. Adults (18–64): muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or greater
   intensity, involving all major muscle groups, on 2 or more days a week; "a strong recommendation
   supported by moderate-certainty evidence". Older adults (65+): "varied multicomponent physical
   activity that emphasises functional balance and strength training" at moderate or greater intensity
   on 3 or more days a week, strong recommendation.
3. **Population fit:** general adult population; the article uses it only as the health minimum.
4. **Provenance of numbers:** the day counts are the guideline's own.
5. **Funding / interests:** Public Health Agency of Canada and the Government of Norway; competing
   interests "None declared".
6. **Superseded:** no newer WHO physical-activity guideline found.

## [2] Iversen et al. 2021 — time-efficient training (narrative review)

Iversen VM, Norum M, Schoenfeld BJ, Fimland MS. Sports Med 2021;51(10):2079–2095.
doi:10.1007/s40279-021-01490-1. PMID 34125411; PMCID PMC8449772.

1. **Exists:** yes.
2. **Supports the claims:** yes, see the claims table. Key passages: Key Points (multi-joint, at least
   4 weekly sets per muscle at 6–15 RM; supersets, drop sets and rest-pause roughly halve time while
   maintaining volume; exercise-specific warm-ups); Sets/Frequency section (10+ weekly sets may further
   improve hypertrophy; frequency matters little at matched volume and mainly allows more volume);
   Rest section (1–2 min untrained, ≥2 min trained); Drop sets section (not advised for heavy compound
   free-weight lifts like squats); summary of advanced techniques (probably better for hypertrophy than
   maximal strength; limited evidence); Maintenance section (Graves et al.; Bickel et al.).
3. **Population fit:** the review states most included studies used untrained or recreationally active
   people; the article says so.
4. **Provenance of numbers:** all figures are the review's own (4 sets, 6–15 RM, rest times, "roughly
   halve"; 10 weeks and 32 weeks from the two maintenance studies it describes).
5. **Funding / interests:** open-access funding from NTNU, no external funding; all four authors
   declared no conflicts. (Schoenfeld also co-authored [4] and [5]; noted, not a conflict.)
6. **Superseded:** no; a 2022 letter (Carlson, Smith, Fisher) comments on it, which does not retract
   any recommendation used here.

## [3] Ramos-Campo et al. 2024 — split vs full-body meta-analysis

Ramos-Campo DJ, Benito-Peinado PJ, Andreu-Caravaca L, Rojo-Tirado MA, Rubio-Arias JÁ.
J Strength Cond Res 2024;38(7):1330–1340. doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000004774. PMID 38595233.

1. **Exists:** yes (PubMed record; author list, volume, pages and DOI from it).
2. **Supports the claims:** yes, from the abstract only. 14 studies, 392 subjects, healthy adults;
   bench press MD 1.19 [−1.28, 3.65], p = 0.34, k = 14; lower limb MD 2.47 [−2.11, 7.05], p = 0.29,
   k = 14; elbow extensor CSA, elbow flexor CSA, vastus lateralis and lean body mass all
   non-significant; conclusion that with volume equated the choice does not significantly affect
   strength or hypertrophy and can follow personal preference.
3. **Population fit:** healthy adults; durations and training status are not in the abstract, so the
   article does not state them for the meta-analysis.
4. **Provenance of numbers:** quoted from the abstract; units not given there, so none are given.
5. **Funding / interests:** not shown on the record; **missing, not a pass.**
6. **Superseded:** no newer meta-analysis of split vs full-body found.

## [4] Evangelista et al. 2021 — untrained men, split vs full-body

Evangelista AL, Braz TV, Teixeira CVLS, et al. Einstein (São Paulo) 2021;19:eAO5781.
doi:10.31744/einstein_journal/2021AO5781. PMID 34468591; PMCID PMC8372753.

1. **Exists:** yes.
2. **Supports the claims:** yes. 86 enrolled, 67 analysed; healthy young men with no strength training
   in the previous 6 months; both groups 4 sessions a week for 8 weeks; split A/B (each muscle 2×/week)
   vs full-body (each muscle 4×/week); 16 sets per muscle group per week; 8–12 RM at RPE 9.5–10;
   bench press +18.1% (split) and +17.5% (full-body); squat +28.2% and +28.6%; muscle thickness of
   biceps, triceps, rectus femoris and vastus lateralis rose in both; no group × time interaction.
   Ethics approval at Universidade Nove de Julho, Brazil.
3. **Population fit:** untrained young men; stated in the article.
4. **Provenance of numbers:** the article rounds to "about 18%" and "28%".
5. **Funding / interests:** fellowship grants (FAPES, FCT); **two authors (ALE, CVLST) sell clinics and
   workshops related to the topic** — disclosed in the article.
6. **Superseded:** no.
   Note: the paper's text and Table 1 differ on sets per session for the split group; both give 16 sets
   per muscle per week, the figure the article uses.

## [5] Pedersen et al. 2022 — untrained women, split vs full-body

Pedersen H, Fimland MS, Schoenfeld BJ, et al. BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil 2022;14:87.
doi:10.1186/s13102-022-00481-7. PMID 35568897; PMCID PMC9107721.

1. **Exists:** yes.
2. **Supports the claims:** yes. 50 women, no regular strength training in the previous 6 months, mean
   age about 22.5–22.9; pair-matched then randomised; full-body 2×/week vs upper/lower 4×/week, each
   muscle trained twice weekly with the same exercises and weekly sets and reps; 12 weeks; bench press,
   lat pulldown and leg press 1-RM, jump height, bench press power and muscle mass (bioimpedance) all
   improved, no significant between-group differences; six withdrew, five of them from the split group;
   authors list "Higher dropout in SPLIT, mainly due to lack of time".
3. **Population fit:** untrained young women; stated in the article.
4. **Provenance of numbers:** counts (50, six, five, 12 weeks) from the paper.
5. **Funding / interests:** no funding from companies or organisations; Schoenfeld on Tonal's scientific
   advisory board (equipment maker; not relevant to split vs full-body). Retrospectively registered.
6. **Superseded:** no.
   Note: the paper's tables and text disagree in places (group sizes, one jump CI); the article uses
   none of the affected numbers.

## [6] Bartolomei et al. 2021 — trained men

Bartolomei S, Nigro F, Malagoli Lanzoni I, Masina F, Di Michele R, Hoffman JR.
J Strength Cond Res 2021;35(6):1520–1526. doi:10.1519/JSC.0000000000003573. PMID 32168178.

1. **Exists:** yes (Europe PMC record).
2. **Supports the claims:** yes, from the abstract. 21 resistance-trained men; 10 weeks; both groups
   improved all strength tests and muscle-thickness measures; isokinetic bench press at 25 cm/s
   improved more with total-body (p = 0.015); vastus lateralis thickness more with split (p = 0.037);
   authors suggest total-body may favour maximal strength and split muscle growth.
3. **Population fit:** trained men; small sample, stated in the article.
4. **Provenance of numbers:** 21 men, 10 weeks, from the abstract.
5. **Funding / interests:** not shown on the record; **missing, not a pass.**
6. **Superseded:** no.
   The abstract does not say whether volume was matched; the article says so.
